Six ways to misread a VCF

AD is not a likelihood

5 reads to 0 says less than PL 177,15,0, which also weighs every letter’s quality.

PL is not a posterior

It has no prior. A rare allele needs more evidence than PL alone suggests.

GQ is not QUAL

QUAL: is the site variant at all? GQ: is this sample’s genotype right?

Depth is not evidence

20 copies of one artefact are one observation, not 20.

A call is not a read count

Base, mapping and alignment quality and the prior all enter.

Filters don’t fix the model

They choose which calls to look at; they make none of them more correct.

And our own pipeline: the sibling-mode cut-offs (90% / 60%) and the homozygosity windows are decision rules on read counts, applied after calling. Slide 30 shows what they cost at 3×.

The core of it all: \(P(G\mid D)\propto P(D\mid G)\,P(G)\), with care about which reads count as independent · 64

To close the appendix, six misreadings that the mathematics helps avoid. Allele depths are raw counts; the genotype likelihoods contain more information because they weigh each letter. PL values are likelihoods without a prior, so they overstate the case for a rare allele. GQ and QUAL answer different questions. High depth is only strong evidence if the reads are independent. A genotype call is not simply a share of reads. And filters only select; they do not improve calls. Finally, be honest about our own steps: the sibling-mode cut-offs and the window counts are rules applied to read counts after calling, not part of the likelihood model, and slide 30 shows how often they can misclassify at our coverage.
