Judging a variant

Believe it more when the evidence stacks up

High confidence: the sibling was well covered

Five or more supporting reads in the mutant

Novel, with no warnings

The amino acid is conserved across species

Inside the homozygosity peak

The gene’s biology fits (check ZFIN)

Look at it yourself in IGV

  1. Open igv.org/app, choose Genome → Zebrafish (GRCz11)
  2. Load the .bam and .bai from results/alignments
  3. Type the location, e.g. chr7:29157506
  4. A real variant shows the same letter in many reads, on both strands

Then confirm at the bench: PCR and Sanger in more mutants and siblings.

Warning sign: dozens of variants packed together usually means a repeat or mapping problem · 42

No single number decides. A variant becomes believable when several things line up: good coverage in both samples, several supporting reads, novel, no warnings, a conserved residue, inside the peak, and a gene whose expression or known phenotype fits. Always look at the top few in IGV before ordering primers; it takes two minutes. Real variants show the same changed letter in many independent reads from both strands. The final test is genetic: a causal recessive mutation is homozygous in every mutant and never homozygous in a wild-type sibling.
