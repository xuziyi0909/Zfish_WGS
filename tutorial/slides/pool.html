The values between zero and 45 percent tell us something about the sample. One diploid fish has two copies of each chromosome, so at any site another version makes up either none of its reads or about half. At 30 to 33 megabases the mutant shows 10 to 15 percent, which one fish cannot produce. Could these be errors? No: at each site they are a minority, often one read in six to eight, and they turn up where the sibling carries the other letter, 8.5 percent of reads, but not where the sibling carries the same letter as the mutant, 0.8 percent, which is the error rate. So they are real reads from another version, present in only part of the DNA. The simplest explanation is that the mutant sample is a pool of several mutant fish, a few of which carry a crossover near the mutation. If so, the mutation must lie where no mutant fish carries another version, which is the best interval at 28.75 to 29.5 megabases. tmppe and HERC1 sit there; TJP1 and the stop-lost in si:dkey-1h4.4 sit where about one read in nine comes from another version, so if the sample is a pool they are less likely. It is worth asking how many fish went into the library.
