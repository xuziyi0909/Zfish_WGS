Adapter: the kit’s own sequence (AGATCGGAAGAGC…), read when the fragment is shorter than the read. Paired-end: found from the read overlap.

Poly-G tails: runs of G that two-colour sequencers produce when the signal fades.

Poor reads: mostly low quality, or shorter than 50 letters after trimming.

Step one cleans each read. Library preparation adds a short synthetic adapter to both ends of every DNA fragment. If the fragment is shorter than the read length, 151 letters here, the sequencer runs past the zebrafish DNA into the adapter, and those letters would not match the genome. fastp finds and cuts them. It also removes poly-G tails, an artefact of two-colour instruments such as the NovaSeq, and drops reads that are mostly low quality or end up shorter than 50 letters. In our data most fragments were short, so most reads were trimmed, but almost all were kept.
