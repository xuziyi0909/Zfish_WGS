“≥ 25%” is the rule for continuing; the peak ends where it fails twice in a row · results/plots/windows_1Mb.tsv · 41

This is the peak rule worked through on our chromosome 7. The tallest window, 28 to 29 megabases, holds 2,582 homozygous candidates, so the cut-off is a quarter of that, 646. From the top we walk outwards in both directions. A window is added while it is at least a quarter of the peak height; the rule says when to keep going, not when to stop. To the left, 27 and 26 pass. 25 megabases, at 507, is below the line, but the window after it, 24, is above, so 25 is bridged: the rule looks one window further so that a single low window does not cut the peak in two. 23 and 22 pass. Then 21 and 20 are both below, so the walk stops and the peak starts at 22. To the right, 29 to 32 pass, then 33 and 34 are both low, so the peak ends at 33. Note 35 megabases: it is above the line, but the walk had already stopped. That gives the 22 to 33 megabase peak in the report.
