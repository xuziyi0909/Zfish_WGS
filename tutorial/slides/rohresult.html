The third view uses a proper model of homozygosity: bcftools roh, which looks for stretches where both copies are identical. The top panel shows that both samples share many such stretches across the genome; that is the lab strain's own background, not the mutation. The bottom panel is the difference, and it has exactly one clear block: chromosome 7 from 27 to 31 megabases, where the mutant is in runs over 92 percent of the region and the sibling over only 12. No other window in the genome comes close. This is the cleanest core of the larger 22 to 33 megabase density peak. tmppe, HERC1 and the stop-lost in si:dkey-1h4.4 lie inside; TJP1 is just past the edge. Note the dip at 24 to 26 megabases, inside the density peak: there the mutant has two different copies again. The next view, homozygosity mapping, explains it and narrows the region further.
