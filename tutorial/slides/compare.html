This is the heart of the comparison. For each variant we look at the share of reads that carry it. A recessive mutation must be on both copies in the mutant, so nearly all reads: the orange zone, 90 percent or more. A sibling may be a carrier, about half its reads, or a pool, about a third: the teal zone up to 60 percent. If the sibling has zero reads with good coverage, the variant is mutant-specific; if it has a few, mutant-enriched. Both count as high confidence. Low confidence means the sibling simply had too few reads to judge, which is common at 3x coverage: two million variants fell there in our data. Anything heterozygous in the mutant, or that looks homozygous in the sibling, cannot be the recessive mutation.
