Alongside the eight steps

Genome-wide plots and large rearrangements

Variant density · peak

Homozygous high-confidence candidates per 1 Mb. Peak: the tallest window, extended while neighbours stay above 25% of it.

Homozygosity

Share of sites (4+ reads) homozygous in each fish, 5 Mb running mean; then mutant minus control.

Manhattan

Per variant, a one-sided Fisher test: do mutant reads carry it more often than control reads?

Coverage

Depth in 500 kb windows. Mutant/control ratio: −1 (log2) = one copy lost, +0.58 = one copy gained.

Structural variants

DELLY: deletions, duplications, inversions. Kept if they pass, are in the mutant and in no control.

Ours

228 structural variants (214 deletions), 148 touching a gene. Single-end reads miss many.

Besides the eight steps, the pipeline builds genome-wide views. The variant-density plot is the homozygosity mapping from Part 1: it counts homozygous high-confidence candidates in one-megabase windows, and the peak is the tallest window extended along the chromosome while its neighbours stay above a quarter of its height. The homozygosity plot and the Manhattan plot look at the same signal in two other ways. The coverage plot can reveal large deletions or duplications. Structural variants are called with DELLY and kept only if they pass its filters, are present in the mutant and absent from every control. With single-end reads only split reads can be used, so many larger events are missed.
