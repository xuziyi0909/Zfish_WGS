Inside the density peak, one block stands out: from 24.0 to 26.45 megabases the mutant suddenly has two different copies, with about 40 percent of its reads from another version, close to the genome-typical level. We checked whether this could be an artefact. The edges are sharp, at about 23.97 and 26.46 megabases. Read depth is normal, so this is not a duplicated region collapsed into one copy of the reference, which would double the depth. At the sites that are not homozygous, allele fractions centre on 50 percent, exactly like ordinary heterozygous regions. And in the two newer zebrafish assemblies the genes in and around the block keep the same place and order, so it is not a misassembly of GRCz11. So the mutant really carries two different versions there, most likely because the mutant chromosomes in this line picked up different pieces by crossing over in earlier generations. For a recessive mutation this block is excluded: the mutant must carry two copies of the mutation. One candidate in the peak, btr09 at 24.5 megabases, drops out this way.
