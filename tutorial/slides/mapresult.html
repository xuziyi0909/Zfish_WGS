The fourth view is homozygosity mapping, the narrowest of all. The top panel is the whole genome: the share of mutant reads from another version of the chromosome sits around 45 percent almost everywhere, because the mutant has two different copies of most of its genome. It drops to near zero in one place only: chromosome 7. The bottom panel zooms in. From 28.75 to 29.5 megabases the share is 1.5 percent, the same as sequencing errors, so every piece of mutant DNA there carries the same version. That is the best interval. It holds nine priority-two variants in seven genes, including tmppe R370W and HERC1. The light band, 26.5 to 37 megabases, is the linked region, where most but not all of the mutant DNA carries one version; TJP1 and the stop-lost in si:dkey-1h4.4 sit there, at 11 to 12 percent.
