Appendix · Runs of homozygosity

Homozygosity mapping as a hidden Markov model

Two hidden states along the chromosome: AZ, both copies identical by descent, and HW, ordinary. With allele frequency \(f\):

\[P(D_i\mid AZ)=(1-f)\,P(D_i\mid RR)+f\,P(D_i\mid AA)\]

\[P(D_i\mid HW)=(1-f)^2P(D_i\mid RR)+2f(1-f)P(D_i\mid RA)+f^2P(D_i\mid AA)\]

Switching state is likelier over larger genetic distances. The best path (Viterbi):

\[V_i(s)=P(D_i\mid s)\,\max_{s'}\big[V_{i-1}(s')\,P(s\mid s')\big]\]

How the pipeline runs it

SNPs only. Calls with < 3 reads, or “heterozygous” on a single read of one allele, are set to missing first: one error-prone read otherwise breaks every run. Then genotype mode, each call trusted at Q30 (-G 30).

Read windows, not single runs

At 3× runs come out fragmented, so the pipeline reports the share of each 1 Mb window in runs, mutant minus sibling. Ours: chr7 27–31 Mb, mutant 92%, sibling 12%.

RR, RA, AA = reference, heterozygous, alternate · forward–backward gives per-site probabilities · Narasimhan et al. 2016 · 66

Our homozygosity peak can also be found with a proper statistical model, and the pipeline now runs one: bcftools roh. It walks along the chromosome with two hidden states. In an autozygous stretch both copies come from the same ancestor, so every site must be homozygous. In an ordinary stretch all three genotypes are possible with Hardy–Weinberg weights. The chance of switching state between two sites grows with the distance between them. The Viterbi algorithm finds the most likely sequence of states. At our three-fold coverage one detail mattered: a single error-prone read can make several neighbouring sites look heterozygous, and each of those breaks a run. So the pipeline first sets weak calls to missing, calls with fewer than three reads or heterozygous calls resting on a single read of one allele, and then runs roh on the genotype calls, trusting each at Q30. Even so, runs come out in pieces, so we read the share of each one-megabase window that lies in runs. For our mutant that gives chromosome 7, 27 to 31 megabases: 92 percent in runs in the mutant, 12 percent in the sibling.
