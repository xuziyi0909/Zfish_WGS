\[\underbrace{P(G\mid D)}_{\text{posterior}}\;\propto\;\underbrace{P(D\mid G)}_{\text{measurement model}}\;\times\;\underbrace{P(G)}_{\text{biological prior}}\]

Measurement model

How the reads would look if the genotype were G: base errors, mapping errors, alignment errors, correlated errors.

Biological prior

How plausible G is before seeing reads: mutation rate, allele frequency, ploidy, Hardy–Weinberg.

Decision rule

\(\hat G=\arg\max_G P(G\mid D)\), then filters (QUAL ≥ 30) and our sibling-mode rules.

D = the reads at one position · G = a genotype: 0/0 (reference), 0/1 (one copy), 1/1 (two copies) · 62

Calling is not one formula but a chain. The aligner places reads, samtools sorts and cleans them, mpileup stacks them at each position and computes genotype likelihoods, and bcftools call turns those into genotypes and a site quality. Everything rests on Bayes’ rule. The likelihood, P of D given G, is the measurement model: how compatible the reads are with each genotype, given what we know about sequencing and alignment errors. The prior, P of G, is biology: how often positions differ at all. Their product, normalised, is the posterior. Then come decision rules: pick the most probable genotype, apply filters, and finally our own sibling-mode rules. Keeping these three layers apart avoids most misreadings of a VCF.
